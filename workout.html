Six routines built to be typed straight into Hevy. Everything is pointed at the same silhouette: wide delts, full upper chest, hard abs, and just enough lat to finish the V. Back is deliberately light — one real pulling day plus a lat finisher. Legs get one honest, brutal session and then stay out of the way.

The same six routines carry you from maintenance through the winter bulk to a cut. Flip the phase and the food, the cardio and the volume dial move with it.

Building it in Hevy

Six routines, one folder

Make a folder called V-Taper and add six routines named as below. Tick each exercise off as you enter it — the checkmarks save in this browser.

Exercise

Names use Hevy's Movement (Equipment) convention, so search the first word or two. If your gym or the library doesn't have the exact machine, take the closest match — the note under each line says what the exercise has to do, which is what actually matters.

Sets & reps

Enter the set count, then set the rep range in the routine builder. Log ramp-up sets as warm-up sets so they don't pollute your working volume or your PR history.

Rest timer

Set it per exercise — the values here aren't decoration. Three minutes on a heavy press is the difference between progressive overload and cardio with dumbbells.

Notes

Paste the cue into the exercise note field and put the RIR target at the end of it. If you have RPE logging switched on, RIR 1 is RPE 9 and RIR 0 is RPE 10.

Back is down to 14 sets — one pulling day of ten, plus four lat sets on Saturday. That's still enough to widen, because the taper is now being built from the top: 17 side-delt sets and 10 rear-delt sets move shoulder-to-waist ratio further than another row ever will.

Traps get nothing direct, on purpose. Heavy shrugging fills the gap between neck and delt and visually narrows the shoulder line — the one muscle on this physique where growth works against you. They'll get plenty from the pulls.

How it progresses

Double progression

Anchors

The lift marked ANCHOR is the one you chase. Hit the top of the rep range on every set at RIR 1 and add load next time — 5 lb upper body, 10 lb lower. Then climb the range again.

Everything else

Add reps before load. When all sets sit at the top of the range and the pump is still there, take the smallest jump the stack allows and rebuild.

Let Hevy do the work

Your last session's numbers show up as targets. Beat one of them — a rep or a plate — on every working set. If you can't, that's data, not failure.

When you stall

Two sessions with no progress on an anchor means recovery, not exercise selection. Sleep and food first, then swap the variation.

Weeks 1–3Accumulate

RIR 2 falling to 1. Skip the drop sets and myo-reps. Add reps, add load, keep every session repeatable.

Weeks 4–6Intensify

RIR 1 falling to 0 on final sets. Every intensity technique in the notes is live. This is where it bites.

Week 7Deload

Half the sets, same loads, RIR 3–4. Keep cardio and posing. Duplicate the routines in Hevy and halve them.

Week 8+Reset

Swap each anchor for its cousin — hack squat to pendulum, incline dumbbell to incline barbell — and run it again.

The next six months

You are here → winter bulk
Aug – Oct · Now Sharpen

Maintenance. Three jobs: start the bulk lean, build the work capacity to survive 133 sets a week, and get honest baseline numbers into Hevy so winter has something to beat.

If you're carrying more than about 15%, a short six-week mini-cut now is the highest-leverage thing you can do — a leaner start means more of the surplus goes to muscle and the bulk runs longer before it turns into a cut.

Nov – Mar Build

The bulk. Full volume, aggressive anchors, and this is where the weak-point block goes — pick one, give it 4–6 extra sets, run it for two full blocks.

Everything you want on stage or on a beach is bought here. Nothing about the routines changes; the food does.

Apr onward Reveal

The cut. Same six routines, same loads, less food and more cardio. If there's a show, count back 16–20 weeks from the date and start there.

The training doesn't change much across those three. Volume dials down a notch when you aren't eating for it and everything else stays put — which is the point of running one program through all three phases instead of chasing a new split every time your calories move.

Fuel, cardio & the dial

Switches with the phase above

Fuel

Maintenance. Eat at your true maintenance and hold weight within a two-pound band across a whole month. Protein 1 g per pound, don't drop it. Most people think they're maintaining while drifting up a pound a month — weigh daily, judge on the weekly average.

Bulk. Add roughly 250–350 calories over maintenance, mostly carbs. Target 0.25–0.5 lb a week — about 2 lb a month, no more. Faster than that and you're buying fat you'll pay for in April. Recheck the average every two weeks and adjust by 100–150 calories.

Prep. Give yourself 16–20 weeks. Aim for 0.5–1% of bodyweight a week; hold protein, pull carbs and fats. Keep carbs highest on Monday and Friday — those are the two sessions worth protecting.

Cardio

Maintenance. 2–3 sessions of 20–25 minutes easy work and 8–10k steps a day. Keeps conditioning and appetite honest without eating into recovery.

Bulk. Keep it — 2–3 × 20 minutes, minimum. Cutting cardio entirely is the classic winter mistake: you lose the conditioning base, your appetite regulation goes, and April's cut starts from a worse place with no room left to add cardio as a lever.

Prep. Start at 3 × 30 min steady-state and add sessions before you cut calories again — up to 5–6 × 30–45 min plus one or two short bike or sled intervals. 10–14k steps, tracked daily.

The volume dial

Maintenance. Run the trimmed week — last exercise off each routine, about 112 sets. You can't recover from maximum volume on maintenance calories, and you don't need to. Hold your loads, sharpen technique, and build the tolerance for what's coming.

Bulk. Full week, all 133 sets, plus the weak-point block on top. This is the only phase where you should be adding volume, because it's the only one where you can recover from it.

Prep. Hold the sets and hold the loads — dropping either is how you diet away muscle. What comes out is the intensity techniques: drops and myo-reps go first as recovery falls.

What to expect

Maintenance. Anchors will creep, not climb. Winning here looks like an extra rep or cleaner execution at the same weight — if you're adding plates every week on maintenance calories, you were undertrained, and enjoy it while it lasts.

Bulk. Real load progression on every anchor, most weeks. If the bar isn't moving by week three of a surplus, the problem is sleep or the surplus is smaller than you think.

Prep. Strength holds for the first half, then slides — that's normal and not a reason to add food. Judge the phase on the mirror and the scale trend, not on your top set.

The waist

No weighted side bends, no loaded twists, no heavy carries — all three thicken the obliques and every millimetre there costs you taper. Keep direct ab work flexion-based, which is what all twelve sets here are. Add five minutes of stomach vacuums most days: it trains the transverse to hold the midsection flat, and it's the same control you need holding a pose.

This matters more in a bulk than anywhere else. A winter of heavy eating plus loaded oblique work is how a good taper quietly turns into a blocky one.

Abs actually showing

Twelve sets a week will build them. Whether anyone sees them is a kitchen question — for most guys the top row appears in the low teens body-fat and the bottom row somewhere under 10%. Train them like a muscle, with load and progression, and let the diet reveal them.

Expect them to go quiet over the winter. Build them anyway — abs you didn't train in the bulk are abs you can't reveal in the cut.

Posing

10 minutes, three times a week — quarter turns and the relaxed front. It's cheap now, and it exposes weak points a mirror won't. Do it before the bulk so you know what to spend the winter on.

Keep 10 minutes twice a week even while you're covered. It's a skill that decays, and it's the cheapest way to notice a lagging body part while there's still time to grow it.

15–20 minutes daily once you're inside 16 weeks, plus two filmed run-throughs a week from the front and both sides. Holding a pose is trained, not natural — conditioning you can't present doesn't score.

Adjusting it

Read before you change anything
  • Only five days? Drop Saturday and move the single-arm pulldown onto Wednesday. Never drop Monday or Friday — those two are the program.
  • Shoulders sore or clicking? Cut the machine shoulder press on Friday first, then the dips. Lateral raises stay.
  • Chest not growing? It isn't volume at 23 sets — it's range of motion. Go lighter on the inclines and get a genuine stretch at the bottom of every single rep.
  • Lats still lagging after a block? Add back the second pulling day rather than piling more onto Wednesday — but only once shoulders and chest are where you want them.
  • Not recovering? The full week is ~133 hard sets. Cut the last exercise from each routine before you cut a day; that puts it near 112 and usually fixes things inside two weeks. On maintenance calories that trimmed version isn't a fallback — it's the plan.
  • Build the routines at full length anyway. Enter all 133 sets into Hevy now and skip the last exercise while you're maintaining. When the surplus starts in November you just stop skipping, instead of rebuilding six routines in the app.
  • One weak point per block. Add 4–6 sets to it and take those sets out of your best body part. Chasing three at once means recovering from none of them.
  • Warm up properly. Five minutes general, then two or three ramping sets on the first exercise of each muscle — logged as warm-up sets so Hevy keeps them out of your volume.
