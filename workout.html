Push, pull, legs, rest — on repeat, with the rest day rotating through the calendar rather than sitting on a Sunday. Two turns of the cycle make eight days, and the two halves aren't identical: the second leg slot is a delt, arm and ab day instead, so legs come around once every eight days and everything pointed at the silhouette comes around twice.

Same six routines from maintenance through the winter bulk to a cut. Flip the phase and the food, the cardio and the volume dial move with it.

Run A1 → A2 → A3 → rest → B1 → B2 → B3 → rest, then start again. Six sessions every eight days works out to about five and a quarter a week, and the rest day drifts — which is the trade for never having to ask what day it is.

Straight P/P/L/rest would put legs on every fourth day, roughly twice a week. Turning the second leg slot into a delt-arm-ab day is what keeps legs to the single hard session you asked for while still giving push and pull two turns each.

Building it in Hevy

Six routines, one folder

Make a folder called V-Taper and add the six routines below, named with their slot codes so the folder sorts into cycle order — A1 Push, A2 Pull, and so on. Then just run the next one down the list each time you train and let the rotation take care of itself. Tick each exercise off as you enter it; the checkmarks save in this browser.

Exercise

Names use Hevy's Movement (Equipment) convention, so search the first word or two. If your gym or the library doesn't have the exact machine, take the closest match — the note under each line says what the exercise has to do, which is what actually matters.

Sets & reps

Enter the set count, then set the rep range in the routine builder. Log ramp-up sets as warm-up sets so they don't pollute your working volume or your PR history.

Rest timer

Set it per exercise — the values here aren't decoration. Three minutes on a heavy press is the difference between progressive overload and cardio with dumbbells.

Notes

Paste the cue into the exercise note field and put the RIR target at the end of it. If you have RPE logging switched on, RIR 1 is RPE 9 and RIR 0 is RPE 10.

The cycle runs 135 hard sets over eight days, which averages to about 118 a week — a touch lighter than the six-day week it replaces, because five and a quarter sessions is what an eight-day cycle buys you. Every muscle that matters landed within a set or two of where it was.

Back is the one that moved, from 14 up to 15, because PPL hands pull two slots per cycle and there's a floor below which the second one becomes junk. If it starts fighting your recovery, thin A2 rather than dropping the session.

Legs at 9 quad sets a week is a maintenance dose, not a growth dose — which is what you asked for. Worth knowing going into a bulk: this split will not grow your legs, it will keep them.

How it progresses

Double progression

Anchors

The lift marked ANCHOR is the one you chase. Hit the top of the rep range on every set at RIR 1 and add load next time — 5 lb upper body, 10 lb lower. Then climb the range again.

Everything else

Add reps before load. When all sets sit at the top of the range and the pump is still there, take the smallest jump the stack allows and rebuild.

Count cycles, not weeks

A block is six cycles, not six weeks — roughly seven weeks of calendar. Three cycles accumulating, three intensifying, then deload. Hevy's history is your calendar; the wall one isn't.

When you stall

Two turns of the cycle with no progress on an anchor means recovery, not exercise selection. Sleep and food first, then swap the variation.

Cycles 1–3Accumulate

RIR 2 falling to 1. Skip the drop sets and myo-reps. Add reps, add load, keep every session repeatable.

Cycles 4–6Intensify

RIR 1 falling to 0 on final sets. Every intensity technique in the notes is live. This is where it bites.

Cycle 7Deload

Half the sets, same loads, RIR 3–4. Keep cardio and posing. Duplicate the routines in Hevy and halve them.

Cycle 8+Reset

Swap each anchor for its cousin — hack squat to pendulum, incline dumbbell to incline barbell — and run it again.

Maintenance. Three jobs: start the bulk lean, build the work capacity to survive the full cycle, and get honest baseline numbers into Hevy so winter has something to beat.

If you're carrying more than about 15%, a short six-week mini-cut now is the highest-leverage thing you can do — a leaner start means more of the surplus goes to muscle and the bulk runs longer before it turns into a cut.

The bulk. Full volume, aggressive anchors, and this is where the weak-point block goes — pick one, give it 4–6 extra sets, run it for two full blocks.

Everything you want on stage or on a beach is bought here. Nothing about the routines changes; the food does.

Apr onward Reveal

The cut. Same six routines, same loads, less food and more cardio. If there's a show, count back 16–20 weeks from the date and start there.

Fuel, cardio & the dial

Switches with the phase above

Fuel

Maintenance. Eat at your true maintenance and hold weight within a two-pound band across a whole month. Protein 1 g per pound, don't drop it. Most people think they're maintaining while drifting up a pound a month — weigh daily, judge on the weekly average.

Bulk. Add roughly 250–350 calories over maintenance, mostly carbs. Target 0.25–0.5 lb a week — about 2 lb a month, no more. Faster than that and you're buying fat you'll pay for in April. Recheck the average every two weeks and adjust by 100–150 calories.

Prep. Give yourself 16–20 weeks. Aim for 0.5–1% of bodyweight a week; hold protein, pull carbs and fats. Put your highest-carb days on B1 and A1 — those are the two sessions worth protecting.

Cardio

Maintenance. 2–3 sessions of 20–25 minutes easy work and 8–10k steps a day. The two rest slots in each cycle are the obvious home for it.

Bulk. Keep it — 2–3 × 20 minutes, minimum. Cutting cardio entirely is the classic winter mistake: you lose the conditioning base, your appetite regulation goes, and April's cut starts from a worse place with no room left to add cardio as a lever.

Prep. Start at 3 × 30 min steady-state and add sessions before you cut calories again — up to 5–6 × 30–45 min plus one or two short bike or sled intervals. 10–14k steps, tracked daily.

The volume dial

Maintenance. Drop the four exercises marked TRIM — that's 12 sets, taking the cycle to 123 (about 107 a week). You can't recover from maximum volume on maintenance calories and you don't need to. Hold your loads, sharpen technique, build the tolerance for what's coming.

Bulk. Everything in, all 135 sets a cycle, plus the weak-point block on top. This is the only phase where you should be adding volume, because it's the only one where you can recover from it.

Prep. Hold the sets and hold the loads — dropping either is how you diet away muscle. What comes out is the intensity techniques: drops and myo-reps go first as recovery falls.

What to expect

Maintenance. Anchors will creep, not climb. Winning here looks like an extra rep or cleaner execution at the same weight — if you're adding plates every cycle on maintenance calories, you were undertrained, and enjoy it while it lasts.

Bulk. Real load progression on every anchor, most cycles. If the bar isn't moving after three cycles of a surplus, the problem is sleep or the surplus is smaller than you think.

Prep. Strength holds for the first half, then slides — that's normal and not a reason to add food. Judge the phase on the mirror and the scale trend, not on your top set.

The waist

No weighted side bends, no loaded twists, no heavy carries — all three thicken the obliques and every millimetre there costs you taper. Keep direct ab work flexion-based, which is what all eleven sets here are. Add five minutes of stomach vacuums most days: it trains the transverse to hold the midsection flat, and it's the same control you need holding a pose.

This matters more in a bulk than anywhere else. A winter of heavy eating plus loaded oblique work is how a good taper quietly turns into a blocky one.

Abs actually showing

Eleven sets a week will build them. Whether anyone sees them is a kitchen question — for most guys the top row appears in the low teens body-fat and the bottom row somewhere under 10%. Train them like a muscle, with load and progression, and let the diet reveal them.

Expect them to go quiet over the winter. Build them anyway — abs you didn't train in the bulk are abs you can't reveal in the cut.

Posing

10 minutes, three times a week — quarter turns and the relaxed front. It's cheap now, and it exposes weak points a mirror won't. Do it before the bulk so you know what to spend the winter on.

Keep 10 minutes twice a week even while you're covered. It's a skill that decays, and it's the cheapest way to notice a lagging body part while there's still time to grow it.

15–20 minutes daily once you're inside 16 weeks, plus two filmed run-throughs a week from the front and both sides. Holding a pose is trained, not natural — conditioning you can't present doesn't score.

Adjusting it

Read before you change anything
  • Want plain P/P/L/rest? Run A3 in place of B3 and you have a strict four-day rotation — at the cost of legs every fourth day, roughly twice a week, and six fewer lateral-raise sets per cycle. That's the trade you're making.
  • Need fixed weekdays instead? Six sessions per eight days can't pin to a calendar. The nearest fixed version is A1 / A2 / A3 / B1 / B2 / B3 on Mon–Wed and Fri–Sun, taking Thursday and one weekend day off — same routines, slightly more frequency, no rotation.
  • Life ate a day? Don't skip ahead — just push the cycle back. That's the whole advantage of not being on a weekly schedule: a missed Tuesday costs you a day, not a session.
  • Two sessions in a row impossible? Move the rest slot, don't delete a session. A1 / A2 / rest / A3 is fine; A1 / A2 / A3 skipped is not.
  • Shoulders sore or clicking? Cut the machine press on B1 first, then the dips on A1. Lateral raises stay.
  • Chest not growing? It isn't volume at 18 sets — it's range of motion. Go lighter on the inclines and get a genuine stretch at the bottom of every single rep.
  • Not recovering? Take the four TRIM exercises out before you touch a session — 123 sets a cycle instead of 135, and it usually fixes things within two turns.
  • One weak point per block. Add 4–6 sets to it and take those sets out of your best body part. Chasing three at once means recovering from none of them.
  • Warm up properly. Five minutes general, then two or three ramping sets on the first exercise of each muscle — logged as warm-up sets so Hevy keeps them out of your volume.
